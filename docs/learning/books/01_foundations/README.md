## Chapter Summaries

### Chapter 1: The First Measurements
**3000 BCE - 400 BCE**

Long before hospitals and machines, doctors had only their senses. Ancient physicians in China, Egypt, and Greece discovered something amazing: your pulse tells a story. Hippocrates, the father of medicine, taught doctors to observe carefully and record what they saw. These simple techniques created the foundation for everything that came after.

**Key Concepts**: Vital signs, observation, pulse diagnosis, pattern recognition

---

### Chapter 2: The Stethoscope Revolution
**1816 CE**

René Laennec faced a problem. He needed to hear a patient's heart, but it wasn't proper to press his ear against her chest. His solution? Rolling up a piece of paper into a tube. What he heard changed medicine forever. Suddenly, doctors could hear the secret sounds inside the body.

**Key Concepts**: Sound transmission, acoustic amplification, diagnostic listening, physical examination

---

### Chapter 3: Electricity Meets Medicine
**1780s - 1840s**

Luigi Galvani made dead frog legs dance with electricity. Alessandro Volta built the first battery. These discoveries revealed an astonishing truth: your body runs on electricity. Every nerve signal, every heartbeat, every thought - all powered by tiny electrical currents. Medicine would never be the same.

**Key Concepts**: Bioelectricity, neural signals, electrophysiology, scientific method

---

### Chapter 4: The ECG is Born
**1903**

Willem Einthoven built a machine so sensitive it could detect your heart's electricity from your skin. His string galvanometer weighed 600 pounds and needed five people to operate. But it worked. For the first time, doctors could see the heart's electrical pattern. The P-QRS-T waves told stories that saved lives.

**Key Concepts**: Electrocardiography, cardiac electrical activity, waveform analysis, Nobel Prize discovery

---

### Chapter 5: Reading Brain Waves
**1924**

Hans Berger worked in secret for years. Other scientists mocked the idea of recording brain electricity. But Berger persisted, and one day he saw it: wavy lines showing the brain's electrical rhythm. Alpha waves, beta waves, theta waves - each telling us about different brain states. The electroencephalogram (EEG) was born.

**Key Concepts**: Brain electrical activity, EEG waves, consciousness states, neuroscience tools

---

### Chapter 6: The Digital Age
**1960s - 1990s**

Computers entered hospitals, and everything changed. Analog signals became digital data. Continuous measurements became possible. Storage went from paper charts to digital databases. Analysis that took hours now took seconds. The data revolution had begun.

**Key Concepts**: Analog-to-digital conversion, sampling, digital storage, computational analysis

---

### Chapter 7: Where We Are Today
**2000s - Present**

Your watch can track your heart rate. Your phone can analyze your sleep. AI can spot patterns humans miss. Wearable devices generate terabytes of health data daily. We're moving from reactive medicine to predictive medicine. The future is personalized, continuous, and powered by the biosignals we've learned to read.

**Key Concepts**: Wearable technology, artificial intelligence, continuous monitoring, predictive medicine, personalized healthcare
