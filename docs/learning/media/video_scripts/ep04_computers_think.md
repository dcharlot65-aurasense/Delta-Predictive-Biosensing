### Production Notes

**Animation Requirements:**
- High-quality 3D neuron models (biological)
- Custom neural network visualization system (interactive-style)
- Spiking neuron animation with temporal precision
- Training visualization showing weights updating in real-time
- Learning curve graphs and data visualizations

**Music Palette:**
- Opening: Mysterious, tension-building (0:00-0:45)
- Wonder/Educational: Curious, bright (0:45-4:30)
- Technical: Focused, rhythmic (4:30-6:30)
- Hopeful progression: Warm, building (6:30-9:30)
- Resolution: Inspiring, forward-looking (9:30-10:00)

**Key B-Roll Needs:**
- Microscopy: Real neurons firing (licensed scientific footage)
- Historical: 1940s-60s computer scientists, early computers
- Modern medical: Hospitals, monitoring equipment, wearable sensors
- Human interest: Baby learning to walk, people exercising, daily life
- Nature: Patterns in nature (fractals, networks, connections)

**On-Screen Text Strategy:**
- Minimal, high-impact statistics
- Key concept labels during first introduction
- Comparisons (brain watts vs AI watts)
- Never more than 5-7 words at once
- Sans-serif, high contrast, large enough for mobile

**Accessibility:**
- Full captions with technical terms spelled out
- Audio descriptions of complex animations available
- Color-blind friendly palette (avoid red/green for good/bad)
- Diagrams use shapes AND colors for differentiation

**Interactive Elements (for online version):**
- Pause points suggesting viewers try online neural network playground
- Links to interactive spiking neuron simulator
- Quiz overlay: "Guess: will this heartbeat be classified as normal?"
- Download: Simple neural network diagram PDF

**Tone & Pacing:**
- Conversational, never condescending
- Complex ideas broken into simple metaphors
- Strategic pauses after big concepts
- Faster pacing during exciting moments (watching network learn)
- Slower, clearer during technical explanations
- Always connect back to real-world health impact

**Script Delivery Notes:**
- Emphasize wonder and discovery, not just facts
- Use rising inflection for questions
- Pause for 2 seconds after major concept reveals
- Warm, friendly tone—like explaining to a curious friend
- Vary pacing: slow for complexity, fast for excitement
