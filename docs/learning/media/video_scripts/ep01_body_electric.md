# Episode 1: Your Body is Electric

**Target Audience**: General audience, 8th grade reading level
**Duration**: 5 minutes
**Style**: Engaging documentary/explainer video
**Tone**: Conversational, friendly, curious

---

## Production Notes

**Visual Style**: Clean, modern animations with occasional historical elements. Mix of 2D/3D animation with minimal live-action B-roll.

**Color Palette**:
- Electric blues and purples for electrical signals
- Warm oranges/yellows for body/life
- Dark backgrounds to make electrical effects pop

**Music**:
- Opening: Mysterious, building curiosity
- Discovery: Light orchestral, wonder
- How It Works: Upbeat, electronic elements
- Measuring: Steady, scientific but accessible
- Closing: Inspiring, forward-looking

**Pacing**: Quick cuts during opening, slower during explanations, building energy toward close.

## Additional Production Notes

### Key Learning Objectives
By the end of this video, viewers should understand:
1. The body generates and uses electricity for communication
2. Neurons transmit electrical signals via action potentials
3. These signals can be measured externally (ECG, EEG)
4. This is real, measurable science—not metaphor

### B-Roll Suggestions (if using real footage)
- Hands moving—typing, playing piano (illustrating nerve signals)
- Time-lapse of lightning storm (opening parallel)
- Historical science illustrations from books
- Real ECG/EEG monitoring setup in lab or hospital (brief)
- Person wearing EEG cap (with permission, respectful context)

### Animation Priority List
**Must Have** (core understanding):
1. Action potential traveling down axon
2. ECG waveform synchronized with beating heart
3. EEG showing different brainwave states

**Nice to Have** (enhancement):
1. Historical Galvani laboratory scene
2. Domino analogy animation
3. Neurotransmitter crossing synapse

**Can Simplify** (if budget/time limited):
1. Ion channels opening (can describe without showing)
2. Multiple brain regions activating (can show just one)
3. Preview montage (can use stills instead of animation)

### Accessibility Notes
- Include closed captions with all narration
- Describe key visuals in audio description track
- Ensure color choices work for colorblind viewers (test with simulator)
- Provide transcript on website

### Scientific Accuracy Review
Before final production, have the following reviewed by domain expert:
- Action potential mechanism description
- ECG wave labeling and interpretation
- EEG frequency band descriptions
- Historical accuracy of Galvani story

### Localization Considerations
- Keep visuals largely non-text when possible for easier translation
- Units: 250 mph (120 mph = ~200 km/h for international versions)
- Cultural sensitivity in any human depictions

### Engagement Hooks
- **0:08** - "What if I told you..." (question engages)
- **0:38** - Galvani's surprise (emotion)
- **1:40** - Domino analogy (concrete mental model)
- **2:45** - "Electrical symphony" (poetic language)
- **4:15** - "You are an electrical being" (identity connection)

### Follow-up Content Suggestions
- Interactive quiz on neuron anatomy
- Download sample ECG/EEG data
- DIY: Measure your own heart rate variability
- Discussion forum: "What surprised you most?"
