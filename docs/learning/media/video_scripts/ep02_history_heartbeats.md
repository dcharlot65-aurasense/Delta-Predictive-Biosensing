# Episode 2: A History of Heartbeats

**Target Audience**: General audience, 8th grade reading level
**Duration**: 8 minutes
**Style**: Documentary-style storytelling with historical elements
**Tone**: Narrative, inspiring, historically grounded

---

## Production Notes

**Visual Style**: Blend of historical photographs, period-appropriate animations, and modern medical device footage. Transition from sepia/black-and-white historical to vibrant modern color.

**Color Palette**:
- Sepia and grayscale for historical segments (pre-1950)
- Red/crimson for heart imagery and ECG waves
- Transitions to full color for modern era
- Gold highlights for breakthrough moments

**Music**:
- Opening: Gentle heartbeat rhythm underlying mysterious theme
- Historical sections: Period-appropriate orchestral (strings, piano)
- Discovery moments: Triumphant, swelling
- Modern era: Electronic meets organic, forward-looking
- Closing: Inspiring, hopeful

**Pacing**: Slower, storytelling pace during historical narrative. Accelerate during evolution montage. Pause for key discoveries.

## Additional Production Notes

### Key Learning Objectives
By the end of this video, viewers should understand:
1. Willem Einthoven invented the first practical ECG in 1903 using a string galvanometer
2. The P-QRS-T wave pattern represents specific electrical events in the heart
3. ECG technology has evolved from room-sized to wrist-sized over 120 years
4. ECG monitoring is now ubiquitous—from hospitals to smartwatches to space missions
5. This technology continues to evolve and democratize healthcare
